# Smart Streetlights and Smart City Operations

From "homes in darkness" TV coverage to a Smart 20 Award, I helped turn San Antonio's streetlight repair problem into a visible, measurable service operation.

## Overview

I led product and program work for CPS Energy's smart streetlight initiative, helping CPS Energy, Dalkia, and city stakeholders move from fragmented outage handling to a unified reporting, repair, and operations model. The work connected resident reporting, dispatch visibility, field execution, and city-level metrics into one operating system for streetlight service.

### Snapshot
- **Client:** CPS Energy
- **Partner:** Dalkia
- **Role:** Senior Product / Program Leadership
- **Timeline:** August 2024 - April 2025
- **Scope:** 135,000+ streetlights across San Antonio
- **Primary outcome:** Turned a public service failure into a predictable, trackable repair program with measurable operational gains

### Top metrics
- **73% fewer calls** to customer support about streetlight repairs
- **3+ weeks -> 1-4 days** for the new repair window
- **43% fewer truck rolls** for streetlight repair operations
- **Smart 20 Award** recognition for San Antonio's Smart City and Streetlight Operations Systems

## Challenge

This project started as a public failure, not a normal project brief. Residents on San Antonio's Northeast Side were going to local TV because whole blocks were dark for weeks. City Council members were getting daily complaints about safety, fear, and basic service reliability. The public narrative was blunt: San Antonio could not keep the lights on.

Under the surface, the real problem was not just broken bulbs. It was broken process, ownership, and data.

### Key blockers
- There was **no unified system of record** across CPS Energy, Dalkia, and city stakeholders.
- Residents had **no simple way to report an outage or track status** without repeated calls.
- Support, dispatch, and field teams were working across **disconnected tools, email chains, spreadsheets, and manual handoffs**.
- Leadership could not quickly answer basic operational questions like **how many lights were down, where the backlog was forming, or which neighborhoods were falling behind**.
- Public pressure from news coverage and City Council scrutiny forced rapid coordination while the operating model was still fragile.

### What was at stake
- Public safety and resident trust
- City-level political and media pressure
- Repair speed and field efficiency
- The credibility of future solar LED and smart-city investment

## Solution

We built a connected service model that made streetlight repair feel like a modern utility service instead of a black hole.

### What shipped for residents
- A mobile-friendly reporting flow on cpsenergy.com
- A geolocated, interactive map of streetlights across San Antonio
- A guided form that helped residents report the right issue without needing to know pole ownership
- Status tracking from submitted -> scheduled -> fixed
- Repair-complete confirmation that closed the loop without another phone call

### What shipped for operations
- A shared operational view for CPS Energy and Dalkia
- Dashboards for outages, work orders, truck rolls, and program status
- Structured work-management queues with locations, SLAs, and crew assignment logic
- Citywide inventory visibility across legacy and solar LED streetlights
- Reporting layers for safety coverage, energy savings, emissions reduction, and neighborhood-level progress

### Why the solution worked
The system worked because it solved both sides of the problem at the same time. Residents got one front door and visible status. CPS Energy and Dalkia got one operating picture instead of multiple competing versions of reality. That eliminated guesswork, cut repeat calls, and made repair performance measurable.

## Delivery

To make the work executable, I structured delivery around one shared operating model rather than separate resident, utility, and vendor workflows.

### Execution approach
- Mapped the resident journey from outage discovery to repair confirmation
- Defined the operational workflow from report intake to dispatch, field completion, and status return
- Aligned utility, vendor, and city needs around a common backlog and shared success metrics
- Connected the resident flow, support desktop, operational dashboards, and field tools through one integration path

### How we managed complexity
- Unified multiple stakeholders around one service model instead of separate departmental views
- Reduced manual re-entry and unclear handoffs by tying status updates back into CPS and Dalkia systems
- Made field work easier to schedule through structured queues, location data, and grouped crew runs
- Rolled service data upward into leadership reporting so the city could monitor reliability, coverage, and investment value

### Architecture / workflow
The resident-facing reporting flow sat on top of the same operational data used by support teams, dispatch, and field crews. Resident reports moved into CPS core systems and Dalkia field tools through a single integration path. Status, completion, and service data flowed back the same way, creating one continuous loop from public complaint to field resolution.

## Results

Once the tools and integrations went live, the changes showed up fast in both operations and public perception.

### Key outcomes
- **73% fewer support calls** related to streetlight repair
- **Repair windows tightened from roughly three weeks to one to four days**
- **43% fewer truck rolls** for light repair operations
- Stronger city-level visibility into safety coverage, energy savings, and emissions reductions
- A reusable operating model for future smart-infrastructure programs

### Business and operating impact
This work turned a public-service embarrassment into a credible service program. Instead of reacting to complaints through disconnected teams, CPS Energy could point to a structured workflow, measurable repair performance, and a more transparent resident experience.

Operationally, the gains were not cosmetic. Shared dashboards, structured work queues, and cleaner integrations reduced waste, tightened repair performance, and gave leadership hard data they could use to justify further smart-city and solar LED investment.
