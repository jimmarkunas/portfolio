# 1. THE CORE PBDS PRESENTATION IDEA

PBDS presentations should feel like **editorial systems in space**, not software dashboards.

They combine:

- oversized editorial typography;
- disciplined negative space;
- surgical PBDS magenta;
- near-black fields;
- one dominant visual idea per slide;
- dimensional atmosphere where it adds meaning;
- native semantic content;
- strong hierarchy visible from presentation distance;
- reusable visual grammar without repetitive composition.

The system should feel:

- intelligent;
- deliberate;
- premium;
- architectural;
- technical without becoming schematic;
- cinematic without becoming decorative spectacle;
- modern without becoming generic "AI" art.

A PBDS presentation should not look like:

- a SaaS landing page;
- a PowerPoint theme;
- a card dashboard;
- a generic consulting template;
- a sci-fi wallpaper deck;
- a sequence of unrelated AI-generated posters.

---

# 2. THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE

## SLIDE FIRST. TEMPLATE SECOND.

Do not begin by asking:

> "Which existing template can I force this content into?"

Begin by asking:

> "What is the communication problem of this slide?"

Design the strongest composition for the actual slide.

Only after a composition is approved and proven should reusable behavior be promoted into PBDS as a pattern/template.

This rule exists because premature templating creates:

- generic card layouts;
- repeated geometry;
- unnecessary abstraction;
- weak hierarchy;
- designs optimized for component reuse rather than communication.

A strong PBDS pattern is **extracted from a successful slide**, not invented in the abstract.

# 3. THE FIVE QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE DESIGNING ANY SLIDE

Before drawing anything, answer:

## 3.1 What must the audience understand?

Reduce the slide to one sentence.

Examples:

- "Ambiguity becomes dangerous when automated."
- "A.G.E.N.T.S. is a productization framework."
- "Each A.G.E.N.T.S. domain becomes a concrete product requirement."
- "The same AI idea can produce either a vague prompt or a buildable product spec."

If that sentence is unclear, the slide is not ready to design.

## 3.2 What relationship must the audience perceive?

Typical relationships:

- one dominant thesis;
- reveal / introduction;
- A vs B;
- before → after;
- framework → requirements;
- continuum / spectrum;
- sequential flow;
- layered system;
- artifact / evidence;
- final synthesis / CTA.

This relationship determines the composition family.

## 3.3 What should dominate visually?

Every slide needs a clear first read.

Possible dominant elements:

- headline;
- brand/framework name;
- artifact;
- diagram;
- orb/particle field;
- number/stat;
- product screenshot;
- transformation moment.

If everything has equal visual weight, the slide usually fails.

## 3.4 What is semantic and what is atmospheric?

Semantic content:
- titles;
- subtitles;
- labels;
- copy;
- requirements;
- framework names;
- metrics;
- diagrams whose structure carries meaning;
- product UI.

Atmosphere:
- particle fields;
- glow;
- orb surfaces;
- edge crops;
- non-semantic light;
- textures;
- environmental depth.

Semantic content stays native/editable whenever practical.

Atmosphere may be image/canvas/procedural.

## 3.5 What will this look like from twenty feet away?

A presentation slide is not a webpage.

The audience must perceive:
- hierarchy;
- direction;
- grouping;
- relationship;
- conclusion;

before they can read every word.

If the composition only works when inspected up close, redesign it.

# 4. PBDS COMPOSITION DECISION TREE

Use this as a first-pass routing mechanism.

## If the slide is a single thesis / punchline:

Use **HERO**.

Examples:
- "DON'T AUTOMATE AMBIGUITY."
- one major warning;
- one major conclusion;
- one memorable rule.

## If the slide introduces a named framework, system, method, product, or brand:

Use **BRAND / REVEAL**.

The audience should feel like they are **meeting the thing**, not inspecting its architecture.

## If the slide is A vs B:

Use **COMPARE / CONTRAST**.

The comparison should be instantly legible. Do not make two generic equal cards unless equality is itself the message.

## If the slide maps a framework into requirements / outputs:

Use **TRANSLATION / TRACEABILITY**.

One-to-one mapping takes priority over decorative minimalism.

## If the slide shows a weak input becoming a strong artifact:

Use **BEFORE / AFTER TRANSFORMATION**.

The result side should usually dominate.

## If the slide expresses degree, authority, maturity, or continuum:

Use **SPECTRUM**.

The visual should communicate continuous change, not separate boxes.

## If the slide expresses steps or operational sequence:

Use **FLOW / SEQUENCE**.

The viewer should immediately understand order and direction.

## If the slide explains layers:

Use **SYSTEMS STACK / LAYERED SYSTEM**.

Depth should carry conceptual meaning.

## If the slide displays a real product/tool/workflow:

Use **EMBEDDED APP / ARTIFACT**.

The actual product is the visual.

## If the slide closes the presentation:

Use **END CARD / SYNTHESIS**.

One dominant closing statement, one action, minimal residual complexity.

# 5. PROVEN PBDS PRESENTATION PATTERNS

These patterns have been proven through actual presentation work.

They should be promoted into the canonical PBDS presentation component/pattern contract only after the normal PBDS promotion process.

---

## 5.1 HERO v1 — Centered Signal Field

**First proven consumer:** PDMA Slide 05 — `DON'T AUTOMATE AMBIGUITY.`

### Use when

The slide needs:

- one dominant thesis;
- one short supporting logic chain;
- one dominant atmospheric move;
- maximum memorability.

### Composition

- near-black field;
- oversized centered headline;
- sparse supporting semantic row;
- centered operator / equation moment if useful;
- centered payoff;
- restrained atmospheric signal field;
- large negative space.

### Important exception

PBDS normally prefers editorial left alignment.

HERO may use **centered semantic composition** when the approved concept requires it.

This is an exception, not a new default.

### What carries the design

Typography and hierarchy.

The decorative field supports the idea; it does not become the idea.

### Reject

- giant random planet;
- generic portal;
- reflective sci-fi floor;
- tangled atom simply because the topic is AI;
- cards;
- filler labels;
- atmospheric art stronger than the message.

---

## 5.2 Brand / Reveal v1 — Particle Horizon Reveal

**First proven consumer:** PDMA Slide 11 — `A.G.E.N.T.S.`

### Use when

Introducing:
- a framework;
- a product;
- a methodology;
- a named operating system;
- a branded concept.

### Goal

The audience should feel:

> "This is the thing."

Not:

> "Here is a systems architecture diagram describing the thing."

### Composition

- enormous naming moment;
- name often centered;
- restrained supporting proposition;
- one major cropped atmospheric object/horizon;
- structured lower semantic layer;
- minimal container chrome.

### A.G.E.N.T.S. proven structure

- giant `A.G.E.N.T.S.`;
- particle horizon behind upper field;
- six semantic columns below;
- thin separators;
- giant magenta initials;
- framework questions remain native.

### Reject

- six cards around a hub;
- hub-and-spoke architecture;
- generic SaaS diagram;
- node map;
- orbital UI for its own sake;
- decorative labels embedded in raster art.

---

## 5.3 Translation / Traceability v1 — Framework → Requirements

**First proven consumer:** PDMA Slide 12 — `TURN THE FRAMEWORK INTO PRODUCT.`

### Use when

The audience must map items one-to-one across two semantic states.

Examples:
- framework → requirement;
- principle → implementation;
- risk → control;
- domain → owner;
- capability → acceptance criteria.

### Core principle

> **The eye should never have to guess which output belongs to which input.**

### Composition

- source system on one side;
- destination system on the other;
- repeated identifiers on both sides;
- clean aligned tracks;
- explicit transformation/convergence moment where useful;
- exact row-level traceability.

### Information rule

Trackability beats minimalism.

If simplifying the design makes the relationship harder to follow, the simplification is wrong.

### PBDS treatment

This does **not** need to become a boring spreadsheet.

Use:
- oversized identifiers;
- thin structural rules;
- controlled magenta;
- atmospheric edge objects;
- one strong transformation node;
- large readable type.

### Reject

- crossing connectors;
- six unrelated cards;
- arbitrary fan-out;
- equal visual emphasis where output should dominate;
- minimal "translation rail" if the audience loses the one-to-one mapping.

---

## 5.4 Before / After Transformation v1 — Idea → Production-Ready Spec

**First proven consumer:** PDMA Slide 13 — `AN IDEA + AI ≠ PRODUCT SPEC.`

### Use when

The story is:

> weak / vague / incomplete input
> → transformation
> → stronger / structured / operational output

### Composition

- weaker source artifact;
- explicit bridge/transformation;
- stronger output artifact;
- bottom takeaway/outcome layer.

### Visual hierarchy

The two sides should not automatically be equal.

If the point is that the result is more complete and more valuable, the result should dominate.

### Proven Slide 13 logic

Left:
- vague prompt;
- neutral/dark;
- weaker visual weight.

Middle:
- A.G.E.N.T.S. bridge;
- unmistakable directional transformation.

Right:
- production-ready spec;
- larger/stronger;
- magenta-accented;
- visually more valuable.

Bottom:
- outcome statement;
- LESS REWORK;
- FASTER DELIVERY;
- HIGHER CONFIDENCE.

### Reject

- two equal generic cards;
- bridge too small to perceive;
- abstract transformation art that obscures what changed;
- output artifact that looks no more complete than input.

---

## 5.5 Compare / Contrast

### Use when

Two states, models, philosophies, or operating modes need direct comparison.

### Rule

The composition should reveal:
- what differs;
- why the difference matters;
- which properties correspond.

Not every comparison needs rectangular containers.

Use open space, large type, edge atmosphere, or one shared axis when stronger.

---

## 5.6 Decision Spectrum

### Use when

The message is continuous rather than categorical.

Examples:
- observe → recommend → prepare → decide → execute;
- low → high authority;
- low → high automation.

### Rule

Avoid converting a continuum into disconnected cards.

Use:
- horizon;
- arc;
- gradient;
- track;
- continuous geometry.

The audience should perceive progression before reading labels.

---

## 5.7 Flow / Scenario

### Use when

Explaining operational flow, sequence, or scenario behavior.

### Rule

Direction must be obvious.

Use connectors only where they carry semantic meaning.

Do not add decorative arrows simply to make a slide feel "technical."

---

## 5.8 Structured Content → Action

### Use when

A slide turns:
- input;
- evidence;
- structure;
- product data;

into:
- action;
- decision;
- artifact;
- output.

This is appropriate for implementation-oriented product slides where the mechanism matters.

---

## 5.9 Embedded App / Real Artifact

### Use when

The real product is more persuasive than a diagram.

Examples:
- interactive exercise;
- production workflow;
- live product demo;
- artifact viewer.

### Rule

The app is the visual.

Do not wrap it in unnecessary decorative UI that competes with the product itself.

---

## 5.10 End Card / Closing Synthesis

### Use when

Closing:
- talk;
- section;
- argument;
- CTA.

### Composition

- one dominant closing idea;
- minimal supporting text;
- clear action;
- strong brand recognition;
- enough atmosphere to feel intentional;
- no new complexity introduced at the end.

# 6. VISUAL GRAMMAR

## 6.1 Near-black field

Primary presentation field:

`#090909`

Black is not empty space to be filled.

Black is an active compositional material.

Large black regions create:
- hierarchy;
- tension;
- premium feel;
- visual rest;
- stronger contrast with white and magenta.

Do not fill negative space simply because it is available.

---

## 6.2 PBDS magenta

Brand Magenta:

`#FF2FAE`

Use magenta surgically.

Good uses:
- important phrase;
- framework initial;
- directional transformation;
- meaningful border;
- active state;
- primary visual energy;
- limited atmospheric lighting.

Bad uses:
- every headline;
- every border;
- every icon;
- every label;
- full-screen neon wash;
- decorative glow without hierarchy.

Magenta is powerful because it is scarce.

---

## 6.3 Typography

Typography carries more of the design than decorative art.

Use:
- Inter / Inter Display;
- extreme size hierarchy where appropriate;
- short lines;
- deliberate line breaks;
- controlled tracking;
- white / gray / magenta contrast.

A title should feel composed, not merely placed.

### Normal behavior

Editorial left alignment is generally preferred.

### Exceptions

Centered composition may be appropriate for:
- HERO;
- Brand / Reveal;
- a deliberate reveal moment;
- a symmetrical closing composition.

Do not center ordinary content by default.

---

## 6.4 Negative space

Negative space is not unfinished design.

Use it to:
- separate semantic regions;
- create drama;
- make one object dominant;
- improve readability;
- avoid the dashboard effect.

When in doubt, remove before adding.

---

## 6.5 Scale

PBDS frequently benefits from oversized elements:
- massive type;
- giant cropped orbs;
- large initials;
- objects extending beyond the frame.

Use scale to create hierarchy, not spectacle.

If a decorative object is large, it should often be **cropped intentionally**.

A fully visible floating orb in the middle of a slide often feels generic.

A huge edge-cropped orb can feel architectural.

---

## 6.6 Edge crops

Approved PBDS presentations frequently use:
- white/silver atmosphere entering from one edge;
- magenta atmosphere entering from another;
- horizon/dome forms partially outside frame.

The crop should feel intentional.

Avoid:
- accidental tangencies;
- tiny decorative corners;
- perfectly centered floating planets.

---

## 6.7 Material / particle depth

The PBDS visual language may use:
- metallic-dot structures;
- particle horizons;
- radial fields;
- signal curtains;
- particulate breakup;
- controlled glow;
- dimensional surfaces.

But materiality must not overwhelm the communication.

The audience should remember the idea, not just "cool particles."

---

# 7. ORB / PARTICLE PHILOSOPHY

Orbs are a PBDS recognition motif, not mandatory slide furniture.

Do not put an orb on every slide.

Use them when they provide:
- visual continuity;
- scale;
- directional framing;
- tension;
- atmosphere;
- a meaningful sense of transformation or field.

## 7.1 Three rendering modes

The PBDS orb foundation is implemented on the active `pbds/live-orb-foundation`
development branch. This canonization package preserves the rendering
philosophy below but does not promote or modify that implementation.

### `reference`

Approved flat artwork is rendered exactly.

Use when visual fidelity is more important than motion.

### `hybrid`

Approved artwork remains the underlying visual truth.

Procedural/canvas layers add only controlled behavior:
- particle escape;
- corona drift;
- rim shimmer;
- mouse disturbance;
- subtle parallax;
- optional orbit/network accents.

**Hybrid is the production-first mode for accepted references.**

### `procedural`

Entire orb generated by code.

Use only when the procedural renderer has proven sufficient fidelity.

Do not replace approved artwork with an inferior mathematical sphere merely because it is more technically elegant.

---

## 7.2 Determinism

Procedural initialization should be seedable.

Why:
- stable visual QA;
- reproducible screenshots;
- predictable composition;
- easier regression testing.

Live interaction can vary after deterministic initialization.

---

## 7.3 Interaction

Mouse behavior should be restrained.

Useful interaction model:

`pointer delta / elapsed time → bounded particle impulse`

A slow hover:
- subtle disturbance.

A fast swipe:
- stronger visible particle throw.

Idle motion stays separate from interaction.

Interaction must never interfere with:
- slide navigation;
- clicking;
- keyboard controls;
- presentation shell.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Canvas should remain decorative / `aria-hidden`.

---

## 7.4 Reference sampling

A future procedural-fidelity phase may sample approved artwork:

```text
approved PNG
   ↓
offscreen canvas
   ↓
sample alpha / brightness / color
   ↓
derive density / highlight / void distribution
   ↓
animate sampled particles
```

This is a later capability.

Do not bundle it into the first shared orb foundation merely because it sounds powerful.

Prove the simpler shared capability first.

# 8. NATIVE VS DECORATIVE BOUNDARY

This rule is central.

## Keep native/editable

- titles;
- subtitles;
- body copy;
- labels;
- requirements;
- framework names;
- metrics;
- dividers;
- arrows when simple enough;
- product UI;
- semantic diagrams where structure matters;
- canonical asterisk;
- header/footer;
- navigation;
- meaningful shapes.

## Decorative assets may contain

- texture;
- atmosphere;
- particles;
- glow;
- non-semantic field lines;
- lighting;
- environmental reflection;
- orb surface detail.

Never flatten semantic content into the background merely to match a concept image faster.

Approved concept PNGs are often:
- composition references;
- QA references;
- asset sources;

not final production slide implementations.

# 9. APPROVED REFERENCE RULE

Once Jim approves a concept:

> **STOP DESIGNING. START RECONSTRUCTING.**

Do not:
- reinterpret;
- "improve";
- modernize;
- simplify;
- add effects;
- replace the metaphor;
- change the composition because another implementation is easier.

Approved-reference implementation is **registration, not redesign**.

Use:
- exact visual reference;
- native semantic reconstruction;
- decomposable assets;
- deterministic comparison;
- evidence-based correction.

# 10. CONCEPT-GENERATION RULES

When generating multiple concepts, vary the **structural idea**, not just the decoration.

Bad four-concept round:

1. same card layout + planet;
2. same card layout + glow;
3. same card layout + different planet;
4. same card layout + different border.

Good four-concept round:

1. asymmetrical editorial artifact;
2. traceability spine;
3. full-width transformation bands;
4. dominant artifact + cropped atmosphere.

Concept diversity means composition diversity.

## 10.1 One slide per concept image

When the user requests multiple concept designs:

- generate one complete 16:9 slide per image;
- do not make a contact sheet unless explicitly requested;
- preserve exact slide content unless copy exploration is requested;
- make each concept viable enough to approve directly.

## 10.2 Concept images are allowed to be visually rich

During concept design, the image may serve as a fast visual prototype.

But after approval:
- identify semantic content;
- identify decorative assets;
- separate them;
- reconstruct deterministically.

These are **design evidence**, not personality profiling.

Patterns repeatedly approved in PBDS presentation work include:

- oversized editorial typography;
- clean visual hierarchy;
- controlled asymmetry;
- giant cropped atmospheric objects;
- strong singular focal move;
- near-black premium field;
- exact magenta accents;

- open negative space;

- visuals that feel architectural rather than dashboard-like;

# 12. JIM REJECTION SIGNALS

Repeatedly rejected directions include:

- equal-weight card grids;
- generic SaaS dashboards;
- architecture diagrams used as framework reveals;
- hub-and-spoke diagrams simply because there are multiple items;
- excessive neon;
- glow soup;
- generic sci-fi scenery;
- random planets or space imagery unrelated to the message;
- over-centered ordinary content;
- tiny dense copy;
- clutter;
- decoration with no semantic or compositional job;
- generic "AI" imagery;
- designs technically on-token but visually unrelated to the established system;
- flattening meaningful content into screenshots;
- minimalism that destroys information relationships;
- repeated use of the same layout across different communication problems.

---

# 13. CARD RULE

Cards are not banned.

Cards are **earned**.

Use a card/container when content is genuinely:
- an artifact;
- a discrete record;
- a product object;
- a bounded comparison item;
- a UI surface;
- a semantic module that benefits from containment.

Do not use cards simply because:
- there are six things;
- the slide has empty space;
- cards are easy to code;
- it is a business presentation.

Six concepts do not automatically require six cards.

---

# 14. DIAGRAM RULE

A diagram must explain a relationship.

Before adding nodes/lines/arrows ask:

> "What relationship becomes clearer because this line exists?"

If the answer is:
- "it looks technical";
- "it fills space";
- "it makes it feel like AI";

remove it.

Use connectors for:
- sequence;
- traceability;
- dependency;
- transformation;
- hierarchy;
- flow.

Avoid decorative spaghetti.

---

# 15. VISUAL HIERARCHY RULE

Every slide should roughly have:

1. first read;
2. second read;
3. detail.

Example:

### Slide 13

First read:
`AN IDEA + AI ≠ PRODUCT SPEC.`

Second read:
weak BEFORE → strong AFTER.

Third read:
specific bullets / outcomes.

If the audience must read the detail before understanding the slide, hierarchy is weak.

---

# 16. COPY + COMPOSITION

Do not design copy and composition independently.

The shape of language matters.

Short declarative statements support:
- large typography;
- reveal;
- hero.

Dense mapped requirements support:
- structured lanes;
- aligned rows;
- semantic panels.

A long sentence should not be forced into a design intended for a two-word headline.

If copy is canonically fixed, design around its actual length.

Do not silently rewrite copy merely to make a composition easier.

---

# 17. PBDS PRESENTATION RUNTIME PRINCIPLES

The existing presentation runtime contract remains authoritative.

Key principles:

- canonical semantic plane: `1920×1080`;
- one uniform semantic transform;
- no slide-by-slide responsive reflow;
- title/body stay in one logical system;
- runtime header/footer/navigation remain shared;
- tall/narrow viewports preserve composition by scaling rather than stacking;
- extra space remains intentional black field / approved bleed.

Never create a special mobile slide design unless explicitly approved.

---

# 18. RESPONSIVE DESIGN JUDGMENT

Responsive presentation design is not responsive web design.

Do not:
- collapse columns;
- stack panels;
- reorder semantics;
- rewrite hierarchy at breakpoints.

Do:
- author strong 1920×1080 composition;
- scale uniformly;
- preserve aspect ratio;
- allow intentional negative space;
- adjust only decorative bleed/crop when needed.

The slide is a composition, not a webpage.

---

# 19. PRESENTATION DISTANCE QA

Before approving a slide ask:

At a glance, can the viewer identify:
- the title;
- the dominant relationship;
- the conclusion;
- the reading direction?

Then inspect detail.

A good slide should work in layers.

---

# 20. THE PBDS EDITORIAL BALANCE

A useful internal test:

## Too sparse

Symptoms:
- large empty regions but no tension;
- tiny focal element;
- feels unfinished;
- atmosphere disconnected from message.

Fix:
- increase scale;
- strengthen typography;
- crop visual more aggressively;
- improve structural relationship.

## Too busy

Symptoms:
- many equal objects;
- too many borders;
- too many connectors;
- small labels everywhere;
- no first read.

Fix:
- remove containers;
- merge redundant labels;
- enlarge dominant object;
- reduce decorative systems.

PBDS aims for **controlled intensity**.

---

# 21. MOTION PHILOSOPHY

Motion should make the system feel alive, not turn the presentation into a screensaver.

Preferred:
- slow drift;
- subtle particle movement;
- restrained parallax;
- small shimmer;
- intentional response to pointer velocity;
- occasional ambient rotation.

Avoid:
- constant dramatic movement;
- large bouncing elements;
- UI motion competing with speaking;
- unnecessary transition spectacle;
- motion that changes semantic geometry.

Static screenshots should still look complete.

Motion is enhancement, not structural dependency.

---

# 22. PATTERN PROMOTION RULE

A successful slide does not automatically become a global pattern.

Promote only when:

1. the slide has been approved;
2. deterministic implementation works;
3. semantic/decorative boundaries are understood;
4. the structure has plausible reuse beyond one slide;
5. reuse does not require preserving slide-specific copy or accidental geometry.

Then define:
- purpose;
- structure;
- variants;
- tokens;
- invariants;
- anti-patterns;
- approved reference.

Reuse before abstraction, but do not abstract a one-off composition prematurely.

---

# 23. CROSS-PRESENTATION REUSE

Future decks should reuse the **visual grammar**, not clone PDMA.

A new presentation should feel recognizably PBDS because it shares:

- typography;
- magenta;
- black field;
- asterisk;
- spacing logic;
- editorial hierarchy;
- atmosphere;
- material language;
- motion behavior;
- composition discipline.

It should not simply copy:
- PDMA titles;
- PDMA exact layouts;
- PDMA orb positions;
- PDMA slide order;
- PDMA semantic content.

PBDS is a design language, not a fixed deck theme.

# 24. HOW TO START A NEW PRESENTATION

Recommended workflow:

## Step 1 — Define the story

Build the narrative before styling.

For each slide define:
- purpose;
- audience takeaway;
- relationship type;
- proof/artifact needed.

## Step 2 — Assign composition intent

Examples:
- hero;
- reveal;
- compare;
- traceability;
- transformation;
- spectrum;
- flow;
- artifact;
- close.

Do not lock exact templates yet.

## Step 3 — Identify visual peaks

Not every slide needs maximum spectacle.

Choose a few slides as visual peaks:
- opening;
- key thesis;
- framework reveal;
- major proof;
- closing.

Other slides may be quieter.

This contrast is essential.

## Step 4 — Generate concepts for uncertain slides

For slides without an obvious composition:
- create 4–6 structurally different concepts;
- one slide per image;
- same semantic copy;
- no premature implementation.

## Step 5 — Approve

Jim selects or combines directions.

Once approved:
- freeze design intent;
- register the approved reference.

## Step 6 — Decompose

Separate:
- native semantic content;
- reusable decorative assets;
- local decorative assets;
- shared primitives.

## Step 7 — Implement

Deterministically reconstruct.

## Step 8 — Visual QA

Compare actual render to approved reference.

## Step 9 — Promote patterns selectively

Only after a design is proven.

# 25. HOW AN AI AGENT SHOULD CRITIQUE ITS OWN CONCEPT

Before showing Jim a concept, ask:

### Communication
- Can I explain the slide's message in one sentence?
- Is the intended relationship visually obvious?

### Hierarchy
- What does the eye see first?
- What is second?
- Is anything competing unnecessarily?

### PBDS fit
- Does this feel editorial and architectural?
- Is magenta restrained?
- Is the black field doing useful work?
- Is scale intentional?

### Anti-generic
- Could this belong to any AI startup deck?
- Am I using cards because they are convenient?
- Am I adding a planet because PBDS sometimes uses orbs?
- Does every major visual element have a reason?

### Presentation distance
- Will the composition still read from twenty feet away?

### Implementation
- Can semantic content remain native?
- Can the decorative layer be isolated?
- Can the composition survive one-plane scaling?

If multiple answers are weak, do not show the concept yet.

# 26. DESIGN FAILURE RECOVERY

When a concept fails, do not make random micro-adjustments indefinitely.

Diagnose the failure type.

## Wrong metaphor

Example:
framework reveal shown as architecture hub.

Action:
change composition family.

## Wrong hierarchy

Example:
every panel equal.

Action:
change scale/weight, not colors.

## Wrong density

Example:
too much copy across too many tiny regions.

Action:
reduce containers / enlarge semantic groups.

## Wrong visual grammar

Example:
generic blue SaaS interface.

Action:
return to PBDS materials, typography, field, magenta, scale.

## Wrong mapping

Example:
audience cannot trace A → requirement.

Action:
fix information architecture before styling.

Do not polish a structurally wrong concept.

# 27. SPECIFIC LESSONS FROM PDMA

## Slide 05

The winning move was not "better sci-fi art."

It was **removing the object-heavy metaphor and making the thesis itself the hero**.

Lesson:

> When the sentence is powerful enough, design around the sentence.

## Slide 11

The winning move was replacing a six-card architecture view with a **brand reveal**.

Lesson:

> Introduce a framework as an identity before explaining it as a system.

## Slide 12

The winning move was preserving explicit A/G/E/N/T/S mapping despite the desire for minimalism.

Lesson:

> Information relationships outrank stylistic purity.

## Slide 13

The winning move was making the production-ready output more visually valuable than the vague input.

Lesson:

> Visual weight should communicate semantic value.

# 34. FINAL PRINCIPLES

1. **Communicate first. Style second.**
2. **Slide first. Template second.**
3. **One dominant idea per slide.**
4. **Typography is a primary visual system.**
5. **Negative space is intentional.**
6. **Magenta is surgical.**
7. **Scale aggressively, but purposefully.**
8. **Atmosphere supports meaning; it does not replace it.**
9. **Semantic content stays native.**
10. **Information relationships outrank minimalism.**
11. **A reveal is not an architecture diagram.**
12. **Transformation should look like transformation.**
13. **Cards are earned, not default.**
14. **Orbs are a motif, not mandatory furniture.**
15. **Concept rounds vary structure, not decoration.**
16. **Approved means stop redesigning.**
17. **References are registered, not reinterpreted.**
18. **Motion enhances a complete static composition.**
19. **Future decks reuse grammar, not exact PDMA layouts.**
20. **The audience should understand the slide before reading every word.**
