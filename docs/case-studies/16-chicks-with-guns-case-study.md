# Chicks With Guns — Founding & Scaling a Digital-First Music Magazine

## Overview

In 2009, I founded Chicks With Guns, an online-only music magazine built around a simple question: what would Rolling Stone or Spin look like if they started natively on the web and iPhone instead of print?

With no funding and a globally distributed volunteer team, we built a multi-surface media product across web, iOS, video, and social distribution. We covered arena tours, festivals, and emerging artists, reached national-scale traffic, and learned firsthand how hard early digital media economics were.

This case matters because it shows founder-level product thinking, editorial systems design, multi-surface execution, remote-team orchestration, and early experimentation with monetization and distribution.

## Snapshot

- **Client / Brand:** Chicks With Guns
- **Role:** Founder / Product Manager
- **Timeline:** 2009 – 2010
- **Team:** 5 editors, 25 writers, 10 photographers, 3 engineers
- **Operating model:** All-remote, globally distributed, mostly unpaid / volunteer
- **Surfaces:** WordPress site, early native iOS app, YouTube / short-form video
- **Recognition:** Named one of the top online-only music publications by Yahoo; regular artist and label access

## Numeric Callouts

- **250k+** peak monthly unique visitors
- **10k+** iOS downloads
- **43+** distributed contributors across editorial, photography, and engineering
- **3** core surfaces: web, iOS, video

## The Challenge

- Build a credible digital-native media brand that serious music fans would treat as legitimate while competing against legacy publications with far bigger budgets and infrastructure.
- Make the product work like a real media system, not just a website: content had to flow across web, iOS, video, and social.
- Maintain editorial quality with a globally distributed volunteer team.
- Test monetization early even without a stable business model for an online-only publication.

## My Role

I was the founder and acting product lead, responsible for the editorial product thesis, the multi-surface roadmap, the contributor model, and the overall operating system behind the publication.

The leverage point was treating CWG like a product with multiple surfaces — web, app, video, and distribution — rather than a blog with better branding.

## What I Built

- Launched a WordPress-based publication with category structure tuned for bands, tours, festivals, and features.
- Added an early native iOS app so new stories, galleries, and video could live on readers’ phones, not just the desktop web.
- Built a YouTube/video layer and shared asset library so interviews, photography, and editorial could be reused across surfaces.
- Recruited and coordinated a distributed contributor network across editorial, photography, and engineering.
- Treated access, bylines, and portfolio-building work as the incentive model in place of traditional cash compensation.

## Delivery

### Phase 01 — Launch the brand
- Stand up the initial publication and define the editorial structure.

### Phase 02 — Expand surfaces
- Add iOS and video to create a more complete digital media product.

### Phase 03 — Scale content and access
- Grow the contributor network, broaden coverage, and build artist and label relationships.

### Phase 04 — Test monetization
- Explore ad, affiliate, and sponsorship models against real audience behavior.

### Phase 05 — Capture the lessons
- Translate hard-earned product, GTM, and media-economics lessons into future ventures.

## Outcomes

- **200k–250k monthly uniques** at peak.
- **10k+ iOS downloads**.
- National-scale artist, label, tour, and festival access without institutional backing.
- Recognition from Yahoo as one of the top online-only music publications.
- A founder case study in product, content systems, remote execution, and content-led GTM.

## Proof

- Public recognition as a top online-only music publication.
- Multi-surface execution across web, mobile, and video.
- Strong example of building something real from nothing under severe resource constraints.

## Why This Still Matters

CWG is proof that I can build something from nothing, think about media as product instead of just content, design systems across multiple surfaces, and run a remote cross-functional team without traditional leverage.

It is still useful because it shows founder instincts, product ownership, GTM experimentation, and execution under severe constraints.
