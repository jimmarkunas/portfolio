# DIRECTV Everywhere — Turning a Marketing Promise into a Real Streaming Product

## Overview

DIRECTV had already bought national ad spots promising “Everywhere” access to TV shows and movies, but the actual product was still incomplete.

I was brought in as Product Manager to help turn that promise into a working cross-platform streaming experience across web, mobile, and connected devices. The challenge was not just technical. It was commercial, reputational, and organizational. Marketing had already made the promise, customers were being trained by Netflix and HBO GO to expect device-agnostic streaming, and the product had to catch up fast.

This case matters because it shows product leadership under public pressure, multi-team orchestration, cross-device UX thinking, streaming architecture coordination, and the ability to turn a half-built promise into a usable product.

## The Challenge (Risks / Blockers)

- Subscribers were still effectively tethered to one TV, even as the market was moving toward device-agnostic streaming.
- Marketing had already launched a national “Everywhere” campaign before the product was fully ready.
- Networks, DRM vendors, platform teams, and device teams all had different timelines, constraints, and dependencies.
- Leadership needed to avoid embarrassment from a broken experience customers could not find, sign into, or use.
- The real challenge was making the experience feel simple to subscribers while the rights, identity, and entitlement rules behind it were still messy.

## My Role

I helped define the target experience, wrote the PRDs that aligned 30+ teams, and pushed for cross-device UX parity so the product behaved like one service instead of separate apps.

The leverage point was turning identity, entitlement, content, and UX into one coherent product story that multiple engineering and business teams could actually execute.

## What I Built (Architecture / Integration)

- Helped shape apps and web experiences for browsing, searching, and streaming live and on-demand content across web, iOS, Android, and connected-TV surfaces.
- Drove a unified sign-on and entitlement model so “Who is this subscriber?” and “What can they watch?” had one answer across devices.
- Worked with programming and data teams to connect linear and VOD catalogs into one discovery experience.
- Wrote PRDs and release guidance that kept multiple teams aligned around one target experience instead of fragmented device-specific plans.
- Pushed a practical product judgment: when forced to choose, prioritize content reach and device availability over polishing a “perfect” player UI.

## Delivery Timeline

### Phase 01 — Diagnose the real gap
- Clarify the gap between the marketing promise and the actual product state.

### Phase 02 — Define the target experience
- Establish cross-device parity, single sign-on, content priorities, and UX guardrails.

### Phase 03 — Align teams and dependencies
- Coordinate networks, DRM vendors, internal platform teams, and device teams.

### Phase 04 — Ship the first real Everywhere experience
- Launch across core surfaces with working identity, entitlement, browse, and playback patterns.

### Phase 05 — Establish reusable foundations
- Turn the first launch into a starting point for later streaming initiatives.

## Outcomes

- **5M+ downloads** at launch.
- DIRECTV had a real response to streaming-first competitors instead of a marketing claim without product substance.
- Cross-device UX patterns and a shared entitlement model improved coherence across apps, web, and connected devices.
- The release governance model helped align 30+ teams under launch pressure.
- The architectural and product patterns established here became the starting point for later streaming-only offers.

## Why This Still Matters

DIRECTV Everywhere now reads like an early prototype for how people consume TV today: authenticated streaming, cross-device access, device-agnostic content expectations, and shared identity and entitlement logic.
