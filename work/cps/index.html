Risks and blockers

System fragmentation

  • Teams operated across disconnected tools with no shared state
  • Resident reports had no reliable path to field execution

Process inconsistency

  • Dispatch and repair sequencing were not reliably controlled
  • No queue visibility meant duplicate and missed dispatches

Stakeholder pressure

  • Program had to satisfy utility, vendor, and city leadership simultaneously
  • Competing priorities required careful governance alignment

Public trust exposure

  • Dark streets were a visible, resident-reported civic failure
  • Slow repairs eroded confidence in city services

Solution

Service operating model

One resident intake path, one shared operations view, and one outcomes layer for city leaders.

Resident layer

Service intake

  • Mobile-friendly outage reporting
  • Single front door for residents — no phone guesswork
  • Status-oriented interaction model with clear feedback
  • Designed for adoption without requiring a login or app download
Operations layer

Shared execution view

  • CPS and Dalkia operating from the same workflow state
  • Outage, repair, and field data consolidated in one view
  • Dispatch and queue management in one system
  • Real-time field status visible to coordinators
Outcome layer

Decision and planning

  • Service and cost metric tracking for program leadership
  • Safety and equity visibility for city leaders
  • Foundation for future smart-city services
  • Award recognition: San Antonio Smart 20
Architecture designed for future expansion — not a one-off repair tool.

What changed

From fragmented complaint handling to a shared, measurable operating flow.

Before

Broken from the start

  • No shared record across residents, support, and field teams
  • Dispatch and workflow management spread across email and spreadsheets
  • Repair windows averaging three weeks with no visibility into queue
  • Leadership lacked visibility into safety, equity, and energy outcomes
  • Stakeholder landscape was politically complex and misaligned across utility, vendor, and city layers
→
After

One service loop, measurable results

  • Resident-facing and operator-facing workflows connected in one system
  • Repair calls dropped by 73% after launch
  • Repair windows reduced from roughly three weeks to one to four days
  • Truck rolls reduced by 43% with clearer operational control
  • Foundation established for future smart-city services and city-wide reporting
