Problem Statement

Platform Couldn't Scale

Modere's path to $1B wasn't a technology problem. It was a political one. Every market was building its own version of the product, the original agency wasn't going to fix it, and internal pressure was pushing the architecture toward another expensive compromise. I led the platform push toward a composable architecture and protected the system decisions that gave the business a cleaner path to scale across 10 markets and 7 languages.

The original agency wasn't going to deliver the right platform and Modere's CTO knew it. I drove the partner replacement before the build was too far gone to course-correct, then spent the next 16 months protecting the composable architecture from internal politics that would have turned it into another expensive half-measure.

The business was being constrained by its own infrastructure. Every new market created more custom work than leverage, and affiliate experiences all carried friction that didn't need to exist.

What I Owned

Senior Product & Program Manager

I sat at the center of product, program, and architecture. My job was to hold the composable vision through the politics and stop the platform from collapsing into another expensive half-measure.

  1. 1

    Identified the Wrong Partner Early

    The original agency wasn't going to deliver. I recognized that before the build was too far along to make changing course catastrophic. I helped bring in the right partner, and made the architecture real instead of theoretical.

  2. 2

    Insisted on Composable

    The easier path was a lift and shift. I pushed for composable: a thin commerce layer, Pimcore as the product brain, and clean separation between content, checkout, and business logic.

  3. 3

    Pimcore as the Product Brain

    Centralized product data, pricing, promotions, and inventory logic in Pimcore, rather than baking it into the commerce layer. It gave business teams control over 4,000+ SKUs and 26 global warehouses.

  4. 4

    Aligned 10+ Vendors Around One Delivery Plan

    BigCommerce, Pimcore, the delivery agency, and countless other tech partners were operating with misaligned priorities. I structured a shared delivery plan that kept all parties moving toward the same architecture.

  5. 5

    Protected the Architecture From Political Pressure

    Internal politics could have turned the $1B vision into dust. My job was to hold the line. I did, and that's why the platform could scale without collapsing.
